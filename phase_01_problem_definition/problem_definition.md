## Problem Definition

The increasing demand for electricity in residential buildings places significant pressure on power generation, distribution, and energy management systems. Accurate **short-term forecasting** of residential electricity consumption is essential for enabling:

* **Efficient energy utilization**
* **Demand-side management**
* **Reliable grid operation**

### The Challenge

Traditional forecasting techniques often struggle to capture the complex, **non-linear**, and **time-dependent** nature of household energy usage, which is heavily influenced by both human behavior and fluctuating electrical conditions.

### The Solution: Data-Driven Modeling

With the availability of high-resolution smart meter data, supervised learning approaches have emerged as effective solutions. Time-series prediction models are particularly adept at learning consumption patterns from historical data; however, a significant hurdle remains:

> **Key Challenge:** Selecting a modeling approach that can effectively handle large-scale, multivariate, and sequential residential data.

---

### Research Objective

This work addresses the problem of predicting short-term residential electricity consumption using historical smart meter measurements.

* **Focus:** Forecasting **global active power consumption**.
* **Methodology:** Leveraging multiple electrical parameters recorded at fine temporal granularity.
* **Goal:** To design a robust predictive framework capable of learning the temporal dependencies and non-linear relationships inherent in residential power data.
